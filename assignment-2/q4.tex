\subsection*{Solution}

\textbf{Part 1: Find the standard matrix of T}

The linear transformation $T : \mathbb{R}^{2} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{2}$ is defined by:
\[
	T(x,y) = (2x + 3y, x - 4y)
\]

The standard matrix is obtained by applying $T$ to the standard basis vectors:
\begin{align*}
	T(1,0) &= (2 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 0, 1 - 4 \cdot 0) = (2, 1) \\
	T(0,1) &= (2 \cdot 0 + 3 \cdot 1, 0 - 4 \cdot 1) = (3, -4)
\end{align*}

Therefore, the standard matrix is:
\[
	\boxed{A = \begin{bmatrix}
		2 & 3 \\
		1 & -4
	\end{bmatrix}}
\]

\textbf{Part 2: Determine if T is invertible}

A linear transformation is invertible if and only if its standard matrix is invertible, which occurs when $\det(A) \neq 0$.

Calculate the determinant:
\begin{align*}
	\det(A) &= \begin{vmatrix}
		2 & 3 \\
		1 & -4
	\end{vmatrix} \\
	&= 2(-4) - 3(1) \\
	&= -8 - 3 \\
	&= -11
\end{align*}

Since $\det(A) = -11 \neq 0$, the matrix is invertible.

\[
	\boxed{\text{Yes, } T \text{ is invertible}}
\]

The inverse matrix is:
\[
	A^{-1} = \frac{1}{-11}\begin{bmatrix}
		-4 & -3 \\
		-1 & 2
	\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
		\frac{4}{11} & \frac{3}{11} \\
		\frac{1}{11} & -\frac{2}{11}
	\end{bmatrix}
\]
