\subsection*{Solution}

Starting with:
\[
	C = \begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 3 \\
		2 & 4 & 7 \\
		3 & 6 & 10
	\end{bmatrix}
\]

$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$:
\[
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 3 \\
		0 & 0 & 1 \\
		3 & 6 & 10
	\end{bmatrix}, \quad l_{21} = 2
\]

At this point, we encounter a zero pivot in position $(2,2)$. This requires row pivoting.

\textbf{Without pivoting:} The matrix does not have a standard LU factorization because we need to swap rows.

\textbf{With pivoting (PA = LU):}

Swap $R_2$ and $R_3$:
\[
	P_1C = \begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 3 \\
		3 & 6 & 10 \\
		2 & 4 & 7
	\end{bmatrix}
\]

Now perform elimination:

$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1$:
\[
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 3 \\
		0 & 0 & 1 \\
		2 & 4 & 7
	\end{bmatrix}, \quad l_{21} = 3
\]

$R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 2R_1$:
\[
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 3 \\
		0 & 0 & 1 \\
		0 & 0 & 1
	\end{bmatrix}, \quad l_{31} = 2
\]

$R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_2$:
\[
	U = \begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 3 \\
		0 & 0 & 1 \\
		0 & 0 & 0
	\end{bmatrix}, \quad l_{32} = 1
\]

\textbf{Conclusion:} The matrix $C$ does \textbf{not} have a standard LU factorization without row permutations because a zero pivot is encountered at position $(2,2)$ during the elimination process.

However, with partial pivoting, we can find:
\[
	\boxed{PC = LU \text{ where } P = \begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 0 & 0 \\
		0 & 0 & 1 \\
		0 & 1 & 0
	\end{bmatrix}, L = \begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 0 & 0 \\
		3 & 1 & 0 \\
		2 & 1 & 1
	\end{bmatrix}, U = \begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 3 \\
		0 & 0 & 1 \\
		0 & 0 & 0
	\end{bmatrix}}
\]

\textbf{Note:} The standard answer is that $C$ does not have an LU factorization (without pivoting).
