\subsection*{Solution}

The system is:
\begin{align*}
	x + 2y + z &= 1 \\
	2x + y + z &= 2 \\
	x + y + kz &= 3
\end{align*}

For the system to have a unique solution using Cramer's Rule, the coefficient matrix determinant must be non-zero.

\textbf{Find the coefficient matrix determinant:}

\[
	D = \begin{vmatrix}
		1 & 2 & 1 \\
		2 & 1 & 1 \\
		1 & 1 & k
	\end{vmatrix}
\]

Expand along the first row:
\begin{align*}
	D &= 1\begin{vmatrix}
		1 & 1 \\
		1 & k
	\end{vmatrix} - 2\begin{vmatrix}
		2 & 1 \\
		1 & k
	\end{vmatrix} + 1\begin{vmatrix}
		2 & 1 \\
		1 & 1
	\end{vmatrix} \\
	&= 1(k - 1) - 2(2k - 1) + 1(2 - 1) \\
	&= k - 1 - 4k + 2 + 1 \\
	&= -3k + 2
\end{align*}

\textbf{For a unique solution:}

The system has a unique solution when $D \neq 0$:
\begin{align*}
	-3k + 2 &\neq 0 \\
	-3k &\neq -2 \\
	k &\neq \frac{2}{3}
\end{align*}

\[
	\boxed{\text{The system has a unique solution for all } k \neq \frac{2}{3}}
\]

\textbf{Special case when $k = \frac{2}{3}$:}

When $k = \frac{2}{3}$, we have $D = 0$, so the system either has no solution or infinitely many solutions. We can check by row reducing:

\[
	\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
		1 & 2 & 1 & 1 \\
		2 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\
		1 & 1 & \frac{2}{3} & 3
	\end{array}\right)
\]

$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$:
\[
	\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
		1 & 2 & 1 & 1 \\
		0 & -3 & -1 & 0 \\
		1 & 1 & \frac{2}{3} & 3
	\end{array}\right)
\]

$R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - R_1$:
\[
	\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
		1 & 2 & 1 & 1 \\
		0 & -3 & -1 & 0 \\
		0 & -1 & -\frac{1}{3} & 2
	\end{array}\right)
\]

$R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - \frac{1}{3}R_2$:
\[
	\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
		1 & 2 & 1 & 1 \\
		0 & -3 & -1 & 0 \\
		0 & 0 & 0 & 2
	\end{array}\right)
\]

The last row gives $0 = 2$, which is a contradiction. Therefore, when $k = \frac{2}{3}$, the system has no solution.
